I love my iPad Pro, and have tried reading books on it on occasion, but I ultimately prefer e-ink when I read at night before bed — which is the only time I can really read now; parenting, am I right?

Amazon just released new Kindles this year and all the YouTubers and bloggers that write about e-ink have been talking about them so I thought I would at least pick one up and see for myself. I had no intention in keeping it, or god forbid buy a Kindle DRM’d ebook on it, but I did want to at least see how much the Kindles have improved ever since I stopped buying them. 

### My Current Ereader

![](/content/images/what-happened-to-kindle/E0C187EF-817F-41E4-BA71-6A10A5DBB05F.jpeg)
_Kindle Voyage (2014)_

I currently own a used Kindle Voyage that I jailbroke and installed KOReader on. It has become my most used device for reading. I do sometimes switch back and forth using the Voyage and my XTEINK X4 Pro, using Crosspoints sync which works surprisingly well. The Voyage is one of the best ereaders ever even though it does have some limitations and frustrations that have kept my eye open for alternatives. 

To quickly touch on those frustrations:

1.  Unsurprisingly the first frustration is battery life. The Voyage is old, so it makes sense the battery is aging, but one thing that is most frustrating is that if I have the auto-sync on for Crosspoint sync, the battery is dead within only a couple of days so I have to keep wifi and auto-sync off and manually sync which isn’t ideal but works.
2. The second frustration is the screen size. It isn’t that the Voyage is tiny, and comparing it to the X4 Pro it is actually quite large. But I have been wanting something larger lately due to my aging eyes, and having something larger in the 8-10 inch range would be nice.

Both of these things are not solved by the 2026 Kindle that I picked up from my local Target. The reason I bought the Kindle was to see how improved they have gotten over the last few years. So my goal wasn’t to see if it improved any of my frustrations of the Voyage but to just get a glimpse at what Amazon has to offer.

### 2026 Kindle Thoughts
![](/content/images/what-happened-to-kindle/6CFE45EA-E8F1-40DA-BB39-68BA6B144FEF.jpeg)
_2026 Kindle_

The casing of the Kindles are nice, I am sure the aluminum versions are even better, but I do like that all of them have flush displays to the casing and really think the boxy form factor is better. I also appreciate that the power button is up on top right side of the device instead of the bottom like older Kindles — the Voyage has the power button on the top right of the back of the device which is the best placement of all.

So the casing and smaller changes like USB-C and button placement are nice improvements, but one thing that hasn’t been talked about enough is how bad the display looks. I think the matte glass on the front of the new Kindle’s is part of the problem, but the e-ink itself is so bad that I don’t know why people aren’t talking about it more. 

The contrast of black text on the white background not good. The text is more of a dark gray color instead of the deep black I am used to seeing on e-ink readers especially Kindles. The text also just doesn’t look as crisp to me which is something I always felt Kindle’s shined in with it’s continued push for all models supporting 300 PPI.

When I compare the new Kindle to my Voyage you can see a clear difference in what I am talking about. The Voyage contrast is so much better and text not only looks way more crisp but the black text is also so much more prominent. I tried bolding the text on the Kindle but even then the difference was not much better at all.

![](/content/images/what-happened-to-kindle/3ED9663E-C243-403C-A405-715F53627BAA.jpeg)
_ Voyage on right , 2026 Kindle on the left_

My first instinct when I saw this was to question that maybe it is KOReader making things look better. I know KOReader uses its own engine to render text and the e-ink display so I closed KOReader and opened a book in the older but still usable “KindleOS” installed on the Voyage and the difference was still there.

One thing that I do have to caveat is that the Voyage was the best Kindle you could buy at the time of its release. It was a premium option over the Paperwhites that were sold at the time. The problem is, even if you could argue that I am comparing a premium Kindle, the Voyage, to a base model (albeit the Voyage is 12 years old now), the issue is that the Voyage has the original E Ink Carta display. 

![](/content/images/what-happened-to-kindle/0CB5EA5C-022A-4792-A557-32BF31999826.jpeg)
_ Voyage on right , 2026 Kindle on the left_

The newer Kindles have either a E ink Carta 1200 or 1300 (it has not been confirmed which panel it has but the previous model , the 2024 Kindle, had 1200 and some reviewers have said that they think the 2026 model has a 1300 panel), which means that you would hope to see better improvement in contrast.

![](/content/images/what-happened-to-kindle/C30796F0-7D5F-40D0-8A19-85E12F4BBC83.jpeg)![](/content/images/what-happened-to-kindle/1595C52F-8B4B-43D1-9F50-99A2AB832EEE.jpeg)
_2026 Kindle on the top, Voyage on bottom_

One side note, most of the improvements with newer e-ink technology will focus on refresh and ghosting, but contrast has also seen some improvements over the years, some say around 15% to 20% improvements over each generations, and why I am so surprised that the Kindles look so bad. 

It is really up to the manufacturer using these panels to enhance and make these screens look good, so why Amazon has made the newer Kindle worse over time makes no sense to me. I know many have said the Kindle Colorsoft’s Kaliedo displays are one of the best compared to other Color e-ink devices so they do know how to improve the panels, so why is the new Kindle not better than a 12 year old model?

### Continued Disappointment

Not only am I happy to return this Kindle because I never planned on keeping it anyway, but I am also pleased that my Kindle Voyage continues to shine in a better reading experience overall. Not only the clarity of text, but the fact that Amazon released a really expensive option to add buttons to the new Paperwhite seems half baked and not good, where my Voyage still has capacitive buttons that work great. 

People will continue to buy Kindles, they are a great option for those who just want to read books and have a ton with you at all times. The convenience of having a paper-like reading experience on a device that lets you buy books directly on it will always make Kindles popular. Not only that, you can buy them easily at local stores where most Kobos and Boox you have to buy online.

I hate the word Enshittification, I understand and agree with the concept but have always hated the word, but this is a perfect example of it taking place. Amazon has a great lock-in when it comes to ebooks so it is easy for them to get away with making a shitty experience but sell it as the _best_ because people who own a ton of Kindle Books will continue to buy their ereaders.

It is so disappointing that Amazon lost the plot here. I always felt that Kindles were a pinnacle of providing at least a great experience for ereading, even if I didn’t like the company or its behavior with locking down ebooks, since it felt like they provided great hardware at a reasonable price with the understanding that they will make money on people buying ebooks in there ecosystem.

Now it feels like you are getting the worst of both worlds. An expensive entrance into the ecosystem with worse hardware when it comes to the displays and the risk of not truly having access to your ebooks purchases. Which leads me back to my title of this post; What Happened to Kindle?
