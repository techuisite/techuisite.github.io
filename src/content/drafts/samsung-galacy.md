A few months back I was upset with the big tech companies and them bowing down to our disgusting administration currently in charge of my country. I am still bitter about all those tech CEOs in attendance at the inauguration and donating millions to what I believe is an authoritarian movement going against our democracy. 

Regardless of my anger, I was also terrified in how much the government was going to be given access to our data given how much it felt that the tech companies were doing all they could to butter up the President and make sure he was happy to keep their companies and pockets happy.

I am still anger, and still disappointed in Tim Cook and Sundar Pichai, but I also have accepted the fact that I can't control everything and sometimes have to live in a world that though my data may not feel the safest or used ethically, I have to trust the companies saying that it is. Google, though notorious for using customer data for future products or boosting their advertising business, says it won't share my data with anyone outside the organization unless legally obligated. 

Should I take Google at their word? Probably not. But again, this is the world we live in, and if I want fancy features and software like Gemini or Antigravity their are some things that I have to accept. I don't think any company is perfect, even Apple and their continued push for privacy-first and data being only available locally unless used in a "secure" cloud that is never shared with Apple itself or others for training or monetization. 

Except Apple continues to add more services like Ads in both the App Store and now Maps, which may not use my photos, messages, or other local data to figure out what adds to show, still think it is doing something, even locally on my device, to feed specific ads or information that I may find useful.

Regardless, at the time I was scared for the future of what my digital footprint was going to be used by these companies and having a close relationship with an awful leader freaked me out in thinking our data was truly in trouble. This still might be the case, don't get me wrong, but I have come to the realization there is only so much I can do about it. Sacraficing both my and my wifes (and other loved ones) conevience just wasn't worth the effort -- might be my last words.

This is why GrapheneOS failed for me. I didn't just want to move away from these _evil_ companies but wanted to have more control of my data and communication, but the cost was just too high. Being able to message my friends and family through iMessage or RCS, sharing high-quality photos with those people, and having a device that was reliable and didn't require constant tinkering is a must in my season of life right now -- father of two with a third on the way.

Because GrapheneOS was not successful, and because Apple seemed the safer choice with my data I just decided to move back to an iPhone. I was using a iPhone 15 Pro Max, that I quickly upgraded to an iPhone Air, then a few months later a iPhone 17. Indecisive would be an understatement in my shifting around phones this year, and it didn't stop there which is the point of this post.

When Samsung unveiled the Fold8 last year I was indifferent to it. I have seen foldables before and because I was still on the _Apple-train_ I didn't even consider a Samsung phone an option. The thing that triggered my actual desire to get it was my changing of priorities of what I have been using my tools for, and my boredom with Apple and it's ecosystem. 

I wrote about how I fell out of favor for the Apple Watch and decided to start wearing a traditional watch again with a smart ring to capture some health data. Once the rumors, than the announcement, made it pretty clear a foldable was on the way for Apple and the iPhone, I realized how much I was bored of iOS. Even with a new interface and form factor, I felt that even with new hardware by Apple, I was still going to be bored with iOS like I was now.

So, I decided to just take the plunge. I traded in my iPhone 17 for a Galaxy Z Fold8 which I got through AT&T for free, and have been using it ever since. It has been over a month an a half now with the Z Fold8, and even with some bumpy roads I am having a lot of fun with this device and with Android in general. It is fun to just experience something new and different, but I also feel the whole Apple lock-in to not be as big as an issue that I thought it would be.
